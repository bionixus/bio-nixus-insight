SFDA 2026

What SFDA's 2026 biosimilar guideline means for market entry

On 10 June 2026, SFDA issued the General Guideline on Regulatory and Scientific Requirements for Development and Approval of Biosimilars, Version 1.0 (DS-G-138). It took effect on 10 July 2026, after a draft dated 10 November 2025. Source: SFDA guideline PDF and the SFDA guide page. It is read alongside SFDA's Guideline on Quality Considerations for Development and Comparability Assessment of Biosimilars, Version 2.0 (17 June 2025, implemented 17 August 2025): SFDA quality guideline PDF.

What the guideline sets out:

  • Protocol first. SFDA strongly recommends that applicants submit an outline of their biosimilarity protocol at least 6 months before the intended submission.
  • Reference product. The reference product must have an expired patent and data exclusivity, must be approved by SFDA or a stringent regulatory authority (SRA) on a full marketing authorisation, and must come from the Saudi market or SRA markets. It must not be another biosimilar. Where reference product from more than one source is used, a single-sourced main comparator and a three-way bridging comparison are required.
  • Dossier. Complete stand-alone Quality/CMC data are required (eCTD Module 2 Quality Overall Summary and Module 3), supplemented by comparability exercises at quality (3.2.R), non-clinical (Module 4) and clinical (Module 5) level where required.
  • Waiving comparative efficacy studies. SFDA may accept a waiver where the comparative quality exercise shows high similarity. It does not apply where the mechanism of action is unknown or poorly understood, the structure can't be sufficiently characterised, quality differences can't be justified, or safety and immunogenicity can't be shown from the PK trial. SFDA says its internal analysis of past applications found no case of a positive quality comparison with a negative comparative clinical trial. Waiver requests go from the MAH, with the Annex 2 form and a justification letter, to SDR.Drug@sfda.gov.sa (subject "Waiver Request for Biosimilar"). SFDA responds within 60 working days.
  • Extrapolation. Approval can extend to other indications of the reference product with proper scientific justification and in line with SFDA's patent policy.
  • Interchangeability. Once approved, a biosimilar is considered interchangeable with its reference product. SFDA says no additional systematic switch studies are needed after authorisation, and its pharmacovigilance system has identified no biosimilar-specific safety concerns after approval.

Price caps. Under SFDA's Pricing Rules for Pharmaceutical Products (DS-REQ-082-V03, in force since 14 January 2021; SFDA regulations page, pricing rules PDF), the reference biologic's price is reduced by 20% when the first biosimilar is registered. The first biosimilar may be priced at no more than 75% of the reference product's pre-reduction price, the second at 65%, and the third and later entrants at 55%. SFDA consulted on updated pricing rules in late 2025; check the current version before pricing a launch.

What this changes for entry research:

  • The 6-month protocol window means physician and payer research should start before the filing clock, not after approval.
  • Regulatory interchangeability doesn't mean physicians will switch stable patients. Switching intent has to be measured by specialty and institution.
  • Fixed price tiers make entry order a commercial question. Second and third entrants need account-level evidence that they can win on supply, service and education, not price alone.

Regulatory
SFDA Biosimilar Registration

Reference product: SFDA requires the reference product to be approved by SFDA or a stringent regulatory authority on a full marketing authorisation, and selected from the Saudi market or SRA markets. A non-locally registered reference product may be accepted case by case. Under SFDA's quality guideline, bridging data are needed when reference product from different markets is used. SFDA's acceptance of a non-locally registered reference product for comparability doesn't mean that product is approved for use in Saudi Arabia.

Comparability: stepwise and tailored. The comparative quality exercise comes first, then comparative PK/PD, then comparative clinical data only where needed. Comparative efficacy studies may be waived when SFDA's conditions are met.

For a fuller walkthrough of SFDA dossier pacing alongside pricing and formulary optics, planners often pair this biosimilar entry brief with BioNixus guidance on broader Saudi Arabia market access and pharma market research in Saudi Arabia.

Pharmacovigilance: As with other medicines, biosimilars are subject to SFDA's Good Pharmacovigilance Practices; SFDA reports no biosimilar-specific safety concerns from its pharmacovigilance system.

SFDA performance targets for human biologics (Regulatory Framework for Drugs Approval v6.4, 11 June 2024; Verification and Abridged Pathways v3.0, 20 October 2025, implemented 20 January 2026). These are targets for biologics. Neither document says whether biosimilars specifically qualify for verification or abridged review, so they are not a promise for a biosimilar filing.

Regular pathway: 280 working days if registered with a stringent regulatory authority (SRA), or 405 if not. Priority pathway (40% reduction): 168 or 243 working days. Verification pathway (FDA and EMA approval) and abridged pathway (FDA or EMA) for new chemical entities and biologics: 30 and 60 working days. Those targets start once SFDA approves the pathway, and they pause whenever SFDA is waiting on the applicant, so real timelines run longer.

Primary Research
Physician Adoption Research

BioNixus has conducted quantitative surveys and qualitative depth interviews with Saudi oncologists, rheumatologists, haematologists, and endocrinologists on biosimilar adoption barriers across multiple therapy areas. The consistent findings:

Immunogenicity concerns drive hesitancy in immunology

Even physicians who intellectually accept the EMA's biosimilar equivalence framework express real clinical hesitancy about switching stable patients — a perception gap that determines prescribing behaviour regardless of clinical evidence.

Switching anxiety is distinct from general biosimilar scepticism

A physician willing to initiate a newly diagnosed patient on a biosimilar may still be unwilling to switch a stable patient from the reference biologic. These require separate communication strategies.

Hospital consumption data and physician survey data tell different stories

Our integrated methodology consistently finds discrepancies between what physicians say they prescribe and what department-level consumption data shows is actually dispensed. This gap — between stated preference and actual prescribing — is where commercial intervention generates the highest return.

KOL endorsement is the highest-influence adoption driver

Saudi specialist physicians report that a recommendation from a respected peer — particularly from KFSH&RC or a major academic centre — outweighs brand communication and promotional detailing in driving biosimilar adoption decisions.

Arabic-language patient education is almost entirely absent

Biosimilar manufacturers who invest in structured Arabic-language patient education create a commercial differentiator that competitors without regional office presence cannot easily replicate.

What changed with SFDA's 2026 biosimilar guideline?
On 10 June 2026 SFDA issued Version 1.0 of its General Guideline on Regulatory and Scientific Requirements for Development and Approval of Biosimilars, in force from 10 July 2026. It recommends a biosimilarity protocol outline at least 6 months before submission. It sets reference-product rules (SFDA- or SRA-approved, from Saudi or SRA markets, never another biosimilar), allows justified waivers of comparative efficacy studies, and treats approved biosimilars as interchangeable with their reference product.

Can the reference product be sourced outside Saudi Arabia?
Yes, within SFDA's rules. The reference product must be approved by SFDA or a stringent regulatory authority on a full marketing authorisation and come from the Saudi market or SRA markets. A non-locally registered reference product may be accepted case by case, with bridging data where reference product from different markets is used.

How are biosimilars priced in Saudi Arabia?

How does NUPCO affect biosimilar market entry?
Public-sector volume for biosimilars flows largely through institutional procurement, and NUPCO is Saudi Arabia's centralised procurement, warehousing and distribution company for government healthcare. Winning a tender doesn't guarantee use at each hospital. BioNixus researches the pharmacy directors, formulary committees and specialists who decide uptake after the award.

Are biosimilars interchangeable in Saudi Arabia, and will physicians switch?
SFDA's 2026 guideline treats an approved biosimilar as interchangeable with its reference product, without extra switch studies. Prescriber behaviour is a separate question: willingness to start new patients is often higher than willingness to switch stable ones. BioNixus measures both by specialty and institution.
