Design Principles
Survey Design Best Practices for Saudi Arabia

Under 20 minutes

Completion rates drop significantly beyond this threshold. Split complex research topics into a quantitative survey and a separate qualitative follow-up.

Stratify by institution and geography

Set quotas by hospital network — MOH, NGHA, private — and by region — Riyadh, Jeddah, Mecca, Eastern Province. Without institutional stratification, your data will over-represent whichever physician type is easiest to recruit.

Use SFDA-registered brand names

Cross-reference trade names with SFDA registration names — these occasionally differ from European or US registered names.

Frame formulary questions carefully

Questions about NUPCO decisions or MOH policy should focus on clinical reasoning. Saudi physicians engage more openly when questions reflect genuine clinical interest rather than commercial intelligence gathering.

  • Define decision use — launch ATU, message test, access dossier, or account planning — so questionnaire length and sample design match the commercial question.

Methodology
Survey Modalities, ATU Studies, and Channel Mix

Saudi physician research is not one-size-fits-all. The right design depends on whether you need launch tracking, message testing, or formulary evidence.

Quantitative online (CAWI): Works well for brand tracking and ATU (awareness, trial, usage) among specialists who can complete a structured questionnaire between clinics. BioNixus uses bilingual instruments (Arabic primary, English reference) and device-agnostic layouts tested on mobile because many Saudi physicians complete surveys between ward rounds.

Telephone depth (CATI) and video IDIs: Preferred for complex launch narratives, biosimilar switching, or sensitive formulary topics where probing matters. Moderators are clinically fluent in Arabic medical terminology for oncology, endocrinology, and immunology vocabularies that do not translate literally from US label language.

Hybrid ATU + qualitative: A 12–18 minute quantitative wave followed by 20–30 minute interviews with high-influence respondents closes the gap between stated intent and behaviour — especially when consumption data shows divergence in high-volume MOH accounts.

Private and insurance channels: Physicians in Saudi German Hospital, Dr. Sulaiman Al Habib, and other private groups often manage mixed insured and self-pay populations. Surveys must segment by payer type; otherwise results over-weight MOH protocol behaviour and under-represent premium-brand opportunities in the fastest-growing channel.

Vision 2030 workforce dynamics: Saudisation policies and consultant recruitment programmes are changing department leadership faster than historical panels assume. BioNixus refreshes panel affiliation quarterly and validates institution against SCHS workplace records, not legacy CRM lists imported from other markets.

Data security: Respondent-level files are transferred through encrypted channels with role-based access; sponsor-facing deliverables default to aggregated tables unless individual-level data is explicitly scoped.

FAQ
Physician surveys in Saudi Arabia — frequently asked questions
How do you run physician surveys in Saudi Arabia?

BioNixus designs Arabic-first questionnaires, verifies physicians against SCHS registration, stratifies quotas by hospital network, and integrates results with hospital consumption data. Fieldwork is managed from London and Cairo with in-Kingdom recruiter relationships — not consumer panels.

What sample size is realistic for Saudi oncology?

Roughly 40–80 quantitative completes are feasible for oncology; ultra-rare tumours may require qualitative-only designs. Feasibility is confirmed against SCHS specialty counts before quoting field timelines.

Do surveys require IRB or ethics approval?

Commercial HCP market research typically follows ESOMAR and ICH-GCP standards with documented consent. Hospital recruitment or patient-linked instruments may require institutional IRB or Saudi Health Council registration — scoped in the feasibility memo.

Why combine surveys with consumption data?

Surveys explain why physicians prescribe; consumption data shows what hospitals actually buy. Together they support NUPCO dossiers, launch tracking, and account plans without relying on global syndicated audits that under-represent Saudi hospital networks.

What incentives are appropriate for Saudi physicians?

Professional honoraria of SAR 150–400 per completed interview are typical, calibrated to length and specialty, with digital gift cards preferred for audit trails. BioNixus documents incentives in line with SCHS guidance and sponsor compliance policies.

How fast can fieldwork start?

Questionnaire design and Arabic medical translation usually take one to two weeks; main fieldwork two to four weeks after pilot. IRB-dependent studies add two to six weeks depending on institution. Request a feasibility assessment for a calendar tied to your therapy area.

Which therapy areas are most common in KSA physician surveys?

Diabetes and cardiometabolic diseases, oncology and haematology, immunology and rheumatology, respiratory, and vaccines — each with distinct institution mixes. Rare disease studies often combine qualitative depth with targeted quantitative modules rather than national representative samples.

Can surveys support NUPCO dossiers?

Yes. Physician unmet-need and comparative practice surveys — run with documented methodology — complement consumption data in formulary submissions when positioned as supportive evidence alongside clinical trials, not as standalone proof of efficacy.
